What to notice. Each VM carries a whole operating system, so the server fills faster and every new one has to boot. A container is a group of processes on a kernel that is already running, so it starts almost at once and many more fit. That is density and start time. The price is the boundary: every container depends on the one shared kernel. A container is not a smaller VM; its wall is in a different place.
Sizes and times are illustrative orders of magnitude, not measurements: a VM typically boots in seconds to minutes, a container typically starts in under a second to a few seconds. Real figures depend on the image and the workload.
