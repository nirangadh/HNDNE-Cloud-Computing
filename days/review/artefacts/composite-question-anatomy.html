Anatomy of a composite question

This is the Day 1 practice question, taken one part at a time. Attempt each part before you look: write here, or on paper. Then compare your attempt with what a strong answer covers, and only then read the model answer. Nothing you type is saved or sent anywhere.

The scenario

The south-west monsoon has returned. A substation fault leaves Colombo, including Thara Hospitals' flagship hospital and its IT office, without mains power for six hours. The basement server room is dry this time, but its generator runs for only two hours.

Thara's six-month pilot is being built in AWS in Asia Pacific (Mumbai), ap-south-1. The Head of IT must choose how the patient portal's web tier is packaged: as a virtual machine image (an image of a whole EC2 instance, operating system included) or as a container image (the portal and its libraries, run by a container runtime on an EC2 instance). He also proposes running the pilot in a single availability zone "to keep us inside the budget".

  • The CFO has approved USD 150 a month for the pilot, and wants every cost line explained.
  • The Data Protection Officer requires that patient data stay in a known location and that Thara can say who can read it at every layer.
  • The hospital information system and the Colombo IT office are not part of the pilot; they remain on the premises.

Before you start: which facts here rule some answers out? Say them to yourself, then check.

Three people, three constraints: a ceiling on cost, a rule about where the data is and who reads it, and a boundary on what the pilot includes. Every part of your answer should touch at least one of them. The six hours and the two-hour generator are facts you will use in part (c).

(a) EXPLAIN 6 marks

Explain the difference in isolation boundary between a virtual machine running on a Type 1 hypervisor such as the AWS Nitro System, and a container running on a shared kernel using namespaces and cgroups. State what follows from that difference for start-up time and for attack surface.

Attempt the part first: the buttons open once you have written a few lines, here or on paper.

What a strong answer covers

It places the boundaries precisely. A VM has its own guest kernel, and isolation is enforced by the hypervisor (on EC2, a thin hypervisor with networking, storage and security offloaded to Nitro hardware). A container is a group of processes on the host's kernel, isolated by namespaces (what it can see) and limited by cgroups (what it can use). It then draws the consequences: containers start faster because no kernel boots, and pack more densely; but every container on a host depends on the one shared kernel, so a kernel flaw exposes all of them, whereas a VM escape must defeat the hypervisor. Answers that call a container "a lightweight VM" miss the point of the question.

Check your attempt: did you say where each boundary is, and did you give both consequences that the question asks for?

A model answer

  1. NAMESA virtual machine has a guest kernel of its own, and a Type 1 hypervisor keeps one machine apart from the next. On EC2 that is the thin hypervisor of the Nitro System, with networking and storage moved onto Nitro hardware.
  2. NAMESA container has no kernel of its own. It is a group of processes on the host's kernel: namespaces limit what it can see, and cgroups limit what it can use.
  3. NAMESTwo things follow. A container starts faster, because no kernel has to boot, and more of them fit on one host.
  4. NAMESThe attack surface differs too. Every container on a host depends on the one shared kernel, so a flaw in that kernel exposes all of them, whereas an escape from a VM has to defeat the hypervisor.
  5. NAMESSo a container is not a lighter virtual machine. It is a different boundary, with a different thing to trust.

Part (a) asks for explanation, so every line here names a mechanism and says what follows from it. Thara barely appears, and that is correct for this part.

(b) APPLY 8 marks

Recommend whether Thara should package the portal's web tier as a virtual machine image or as a container image for the pilot, and justify your recommendation against the scenario. In your answer:

  • describe where the portal runs so that the six-hour Colombo outage does not take the portal offline; and
  • using the Shared Responsibility Model, state which layers of your chosen design Thara must secure and which AWS secures.

Attempt the part first: the buttons open once you have written a few lines, here or on paper.

What a strong answer covers

Either recommendation can earn full marks if it is argued from the scenario. A strong answer notices that both options run on EC2 in ap-south-1, so neither is affected by a power failure in Colombo: the outage is a question of where the portal runs, not how it is packaged. It then chooses on the real differences for a small team in a pilot. For example, a VM image is closer to what the IT team already runs and is rebuildable after a failure; a container image is smaller, starts faster and is easier to reproduce exactly, but adds a runtime to learn and patch. The shared responsibility part should be specific: with the portal on EC2, Thara patches the operating system, and with containers also the runtime and the images; Thara controls access, security group rules and the data; AWS secures the facilities, hardware, network and hypervisor. Strong answers link this to the DPO: patient data stays in ap-south-1, and Thara can name who reads it at each layer.

Check your attempt: did you make a recommendation, or only compare? Did the outage, the small team and the DPO each appear in a sentence of yours?

A model answer

  1. APPLIESI recommend a virtual machine image for the pilot.
  2. APPLIESThe outage does not choose between them. Either image runs on EC2 in ap-south-1, so the portal is in Mumbai and patients reach it over the internet while Colombo is dark. The six hours are about where the portal runs, not how it is packaged.
  3. APPLIESThe packaging is decided by the team. Thara's IT team is small and the pilot lasts six months. A VM image is close to what they run today and can rebuild the web tier after a failure.
  4. WEIGHSA container image would be smaller, start faster and be easier to reproduce exactly, but it adds a runtime that the same few people must learn and patch.
  5. APPLIESUnder the Shared Responsibility Model, Thara patches the operating system on its instances, controls who can sign in, writes the security group rules and answers for the patient data. AWS secures the buildings, the hardware, the network and the hypervisor.
  6. APPLIESThat answers the Data Protection Officer: the data stays in ap-south-1, and at each layer Thara can name who is able to read it.

Notice the first line. It makes the recommendation before it argues for it, and every line after it could only have been written about Thara.

(c) EVALUATE 6 marks

Evaluate the Head of IT's proposal to run the pilot in a single availability zone. Consider what does and does not fail during the Colombo outage, what a single-AZ design risks that the outage does not, and the cost argument. State what would change your view.

Attempt the part first: the buttons open once you have written a few lines, here or on paper.

What a strong answer covers

It separates two different failures. The Colombo outage does not touch AWS: the portal keeps serving patients over the internet. What fails is on Thara's side: the on-premises HIS once the generator runs out, and administrative access from the IT office, so staff may need another way in. A single AZ, however, is exposed to a failure inside AWS: if that AZ has a problem, the whole pilot stops, which is exactly the "one basement" risk the move was meant to end. The cost argument should be weighed, not assumed: a second AZ matters most for the parts that must stay up, and the pilot's budget is an argument for sizing carefully, not automatically for giving up the AZ. Strong answers say what would change their view, for example if the pilot's recovery targets were relaxed, or if a second AZ pushed a stated cost line beyond the USD 150 ceiling.

Check your attempt: does your last sentence say what you would choose, what you give up, and what would change your mind?

A model answer

  1. APPLIESThe outage is not an argument for or against the proposal, because it does not touch AWS. The portal keeps serving patients from Mumbai.
  2. APPLIESWhat fails is in Colombo: the hospital information system, once the generator stops after two hours, and administrative access from the IT office, so the team may need another way in.
  3. WEIGHSA single zone risks something that the outage does not: a fault inside AWS. If that one zone has a problem, the whole pilot stops. That is the one basement again, which the move was meant to end.
  4. WEIGHSThe proposal is right that a second zone costs money. But the ceiling is a reason to size each part carefully, and a second zone matters most for the parts that must stay up.
  5. WEIGHSI would keep two zones for what patients use and size it small. I give up some of the USD 150. I would change my view if the pilot's recovery targets were relaxed, or if the second zone pushed a stated cost line over the ceiling.

The last line is the shape to copy: a choice, what it costs, and what would change it.
